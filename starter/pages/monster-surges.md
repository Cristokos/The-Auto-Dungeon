When magic builds up across a region faster than anyone clears it, the spawning of monsters swells for weeks and ends in a few powerful creatures. Surges are the defining civic emergency of the world: the reason towns have walls, the reason the [[Adventure Society]] can call up every licensed adventurer, and the reason old people count years.

**What players know.** Sometimes the magic builds faster than anyone clears it. Spawns grow more frequent, then stronger, then stranger, over weeks. Older residents speak of past surges the way coastal towns speak of storms: in lowered voices, with a list of the names they lost. No one can say for certain when the next one will come.

> *The magic in the delta has been running thicker this year. The old-timers have started counting.* (a [[Whispers|whisper]])

## How a surge goes

A surge hits one region at a time. A neighbouring region can be calm while its neighbour burns, and the wider world mostly hears about it in reports and refugee columns.

1. **The build.** Magic thickens in the region for months. Spawns come a little more often and a little stronger than they should. Sensitive people feel it, and the Society's monitors and the Magic Society's instruments start to disagree with the calendar.
2. **The rise.** Spawn rates climb sharply. Monsters appear in places they never do, often in packs, and the Society starts posting contracts faster than teams can take them.
3. **The flood.** For a few weeks the region spawns more than it can kill. Outlying villages evacuate to fortified towns, roads close, and every licensed adventurer is called up. Iron and Bronze teams hold lines and clear villages while stronger teams hunt what the surge is building toward.
4. **The peak.** The surge ends in a handful of creatures far stronger than the rest, the magic of the whole region pooled into them. Killing them ends the surge. Leaving them alive lets the region bleed for months.
5. **The aftermath.** Spawns fall below normal for a year or more. The region is rich in cores, quintessence and loot, and poor in people.

## What surges change

- **Walls and forts.** Every town built in a dangerous region has a place to shelter in a surge. [[Greenstone]]'s oldest building, [[The Fortress]], was built to hold one off.
- **The Society's power.** In a surge the Society's call-up is law, refusal is misconduct, and the [[Greenstone Adventure Society Branch|branch]] director answers to no one but the [[The Duke of Greenstone|Duke]].
- **Magic in the air.** While a surge builds, the region is saturated with magic. Rituals work better, crafters reach for things they could not normally make, and the [[Magic Society]] does its best work and its worst.
- **Who dies.** Surges kill the new and the overconfident most of all. Every veteran in Greenstone remembers someone from the last one. [[Clive Standish]] lost his mentor in it.

## Running a surge

A surge is a campaign arc, not an encounter. Give it four or five sessions: one to notice the build, two of escalating contracts and evacuations, one for the peak, and one for the aftermath and its rewards and grief.

- **Regional, not global.** Surges hit one region at a time. That lets you run one in the Mistrun delta while the rest of the world carries on, and it lets refugees and rumours arrive from other regions' surges as foreshadowing.
- **Low magic is no protection.** Greenstone's low ambient magic makes surges rarer here, not gentler. Its standards slipped in the quiet years, and the next surge finds its adventurers undertrained.
- **The peak monsters** should be a rank above the party's, or close to it, and should have a reason to be there: a pooled-magic creature shaped by the region's terrain (a delta horror, a desert titan).
- **Causes** are yours to decide. Natural accumulation is the default. A cult deliberately feeding a region's magic, perhaps to power astral spaces for [[The Builder]], is the darker option.
- **Tracker.** Use a 0–6 clock: 1–2 build, 3–4 rise, 5 flood, 6 peak.
:::
