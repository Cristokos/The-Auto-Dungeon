Some powers keep no office and post no notices. Cults serve hidden patrons, from dark gods to great astral beings to things with no name at all. On day one, cults are a whisper: missing people, strange rituals, and officials who seem a little too accommodating.

**What players know.** Not every power in Greenstone keeps an office. There are always whispers: of people who vanished, of rituals in places no one should be, of officials who seem to know things they should not. Most of it is gossip. Most.

## What everyone knows about cults

- **They are illegal.** Worshipping an outlawed god or serving an astral power is a crime in [[The Storm Kingdom]]. The [[Adventure Society]] treats a proven cult as a contract, and the [[Church of Purity]] treats a rumored one as a crusade.
- **They recruit the desperate.** Cults offer what the powerful won't: rank without cores, healing without coin, revenge, belonging. Their first gifts are real.
- **They hide in plain sight.** A cult rarely looks like one. It is a charity, a dining club, a guild of night workers or a congregation that meets a little too late.
- **They leave signs.** Repeated symbols scratched in out-of-the-way places. Missing people from the same street. Monster cores bought in bulk by people with no use for them. Astral space entrances that were never reported.

## Kinds of cult

- **Outlawed churches.** Faithful of gods whose churches are banned, like the [[Church of Undeath]], who keep worshipping in cellars and crypts.
- **Astral cults.** Servants of beings in the astral, promised power and a place in what comes next. See [[Astral Powers]].
- **Monster cults.** Villagers or outcasts who worship a great monster as a god, feeding it and hiding it from the Adventure Society.
- **Blood and rank cults.** Groups that have found a forbidden way to climb ranks faster, almost always at someone else's expense.
- **Personality cults.** Followers of a single charismatic leader, often a high-ranker with a grievance, a vision, or a secret.

## Rumors heard in Greenstone

- A charity in [[The Old City]] feeds the poor every night, and some of the poor stop coming home.
- Fishermen in the delta leave offerings on a rock no one will name, and the monsters there leave their boats alone.
- Someone is buying up Iron-rank cores by the crate, paying well and never haggling.
- A noble's son came back from an astral space changed, and his friends are changing too.

**Cult pages.** Each cult has its own page under Cults in the sidebar. They are hidden from players until you reveal them with the lock or the **Reveal to players** button.

**Running cults.** Every cult needs a patron, a want, a cover and a weakness. Reveal them in that order across several sessions, and let the party find the cult's good works before its bad ones.

- **[[The Builder Cult]]** serves the Builder. It infiltrates institutions, opens made astral spaces and gathers people and cores for a purpose its own members only half understand.
- **[[The Night Supper]]** is the Old City charity from the rumors: a front for the [[Church of Undeath]], which takes the dying no one will miss and raises them as quiet servants.
- **[[The Rock Keepers]]** of the delta feed an old, enormous marsh monster that protects their boats. They believe it is a god. It is getting hungrier.
- **[[The Ladder]]** promises Iron-rankers a fast path to Bronze through a ritual that draws on other people's essences. Its leader is respected, generous, and a core user who wants a way past the Silver ceiling.
- **[[The Red Table]]** is the cannibal blood cult at the heart of [[House Vane]], on its desert estate inland from Greenstone.
- The **NPC generator** can roll a secret cultist as an NPC's hidden truth, with a cover role and tells.
:::
