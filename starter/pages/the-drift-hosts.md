**Nomadic tribe.** The peoples of the great steppe, which runs for thousands of miles between the farmlands of the east and the frozen north. They never stay in one place for long, because the land will not let them. Each Host follows the strongest person in it, and goes where the magic goes.

## Why they move: the Wandering Tide

The steppe's magic does not sit still. It pools in one valley for a few weeks, then drains away and gathers somewhere else, sometimes hundreds of miles off. The Hostfolk call this the **Wandering Tide**.

- **Where the Tide pools, monsters spawn,** and the longer it stays, the stronger they get. A town built on the steppe would sit in a surge every few years. Every ruin on the steppe is a town that tried.
- **Where the Tide has just left, the land is rich.** Grass grows waist-high in days, herds fatten, and monsters killed by the Tide's edge leave cores and materials everywhere.
- So the Hosts **ride the edge of the Tide.** Close enough to hunt what it spawns and graze what it leaves behind; far enough to be gone before it turns into a [[Monster Surges|surge]].

Reading the Tide is the most valued skill on the steppe. Every Host has **tide-readers**, people with magic senses who can feel where it is pooling and how deep it runs.

## How they move: walking camps

The Hosts move on **greatbacks**: steppe heidels bred over centuries to enormous size, slow and patient, each carrying a whole family's round tent on a lashed platform on its back. A Host on the move is a line of walking houses stretching to the horizon, ringed by riders on ordinary heidels.

- A Host can **break camp in an hour** and travel 20 to 30 miles a day without stopping to rebuild.
- Greatbacks are **sacred and expensive.** A family's wealth is counted in them. Stealing one is the worst crime on the steppe.
- Herds of smaller heidels and hardy steppe goats travel with them, along with wagon-cages of tamed monsters some Hosts use as guards.

## Rallying to the strong

There are no noble houses on the steppe and no laws written down. A Host is whoever chooses to follow its **Khan**, the strongest person in it, and it is exactly as large as the Khan's strength can protect.

| The Khan's rank | What follows them |
| --- | --- |
| Iron | A family and a few hangers-on |
| Bronze | A band of a few dozen, with a handful of greatbacks |
| Silver | A Host of several hundred: a true walking town |
| Gold | A great Host of thousands, and smaller Hosts riding in its shadow |
| Diamond | Every Host on the steppe, for as long as they live |

When a Khan dies, weakens or is beaten in a challenge, their followers drift to whoever is strongest next. Challenges are public, witnessed and usually to first blood. Killing a Khan in a challenge is allowed, but the winner is then watched very closely by everyone.

## What players know

- Hostfolk sell monster cores, hides and steppe goods in the market towns along the steppe's edges, and they drive hard bargains.
- They judge outsiders by rank first and manners second. An Iron-ranker is a guest; a Silver-ranker is a question; a Gold-ranker is a reason to hold a feast or break camp.
- Any traveller on the steppe is safest inside a Host. Crossing it alone means crossing the Tide.

## Notable Hosts

- **The Host of Khan Temur Ashmane**, a Gold-ranker with a wind essence who has led the largest Host on the steppe for ninety years. Rumours say he is weakening, and three Silver-rank Khans are already counting his greatbacks.
- **The Grey Host**, a small band of tide-readers who follow no Khan. Others pay them to read the Tide, and nobody quite trusts them.
- **The Red Wagons**, a Host that rides into the Tide instead of along it, hunting at the heart of the surges for the richest cores. They lose people every year and never seem to run short of volunteers.

## DM notes

**The Tide clock.** When the party travels with a Host or camps on the steppe, keep a clock from 0 to 6.

- **+1** for each day spent camped in one place, and **+1** each time the party fights inside a pooled Tide.
- **At 3,** spawns in the area double, and the tide-readers start arguing.
- **At 5,** monsters a rank above the area's usual start to appear (see the Bestiary's ranked variants).
- **At 6,** a surge breaks. The Host must already be moving, or fight its way out.
- **Breaking camp and travelling a full day** clears 2 from the clock. A tide-reader's Wisdom (Survival) check (DC 15) at dawn tells you the current number.

**Moving with a Host.** A Host moves 20 to 30 miles a day. Characters riding greatbacks travel without tiring and take a long rest on the move. If a Host is attacked on the move, the Khan and their strongest fighters ride out; everyone else keeps the greatbacks walking.

**Hooks.**
- A Host's tide-readers say the Tide is heading for a trade town on the steppe's edge, and the town won't believe them.
- A Khan offers the party a greatback calf, a fortune on the steppe, if they win a challenge on the Khan's behalf.
- The Wandering Tide has stopped wandering. It has pooled in one place for a whole season, and every Host on the steppe is gathering to find out why.
:::
