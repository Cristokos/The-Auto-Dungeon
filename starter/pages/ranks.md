On Pallimustus, everyone is ranked by power. Ranks map to 5e tiers so NPCs and monsters slot in cleanly.

| Rank | 5e levels | What it means in the world |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Commoners; player characters at level 1 | No essences, or an incomplete set; most of the population |
| Iron | 2–4 | All essence slots filled and the confluence formed; new adventurer, local contracts |
| Bronze | 5–10 | Respected professional; regional threats |
| Silver | 11–16 | Major power in a city; ages very slowly |
| Gold | 17–20 | National-scale figure; rare and political |
| Diamond | Beyond 20 (NPC only) | Near-mythic; a handful per nation |

**Reaching Iron.** Iron rank begins when a person's essence slots are full: three essences, plus the confluence they form. Someone holding one or two essences is still Normal rank. After Iron, rank advancement happens automatically with level. Optionally, the [[Adventure Society]] formally *assesses* rank, which is a social milestone rather than a mechanic.

**Monster ranks.** Monsters carry the same Iron-to-Diamond ranks as people, so a contract's rank tells adventurers what to expect.

**A species is not a rank.** A monster's kind does not fix its rank. Where the magic runs strong, a creature that is usually Iron can spawn with more magic in it and come out Bronze, Silver or higher, with the strength of that rank and a look that sets it apart: an ape born a silverback, a wolf with frost in its fur.

:::dm
The generators treat CR 4 and below as Iron, 5–10 as Bronze, 11–16 as Silver, 17–20 as Gold and 21+ as Diamond. The Encounter generator and contract board spawn monsters above their usual rank, and the Bestiary can show any monster at a higher rank.
:::

**The rank gap.** A creature is resistant to damage from the attacks, spells and abilities of anyone below its rank: it takes half damage from them. If it is vulnerable to the damage type, the two cancel out and it takes damage normally, and the rank gap doesn't stack with a resistance it already has. The same goes for people: an Iron-ranker's sword still cuts a Silver-ranker, but only for half what it would do to an equal. A party can wear down a stronger foe, but it has to hit twice as often while every blow coming back lands in full. That is why a team takes contracts of its own rank, and why a monster a rank above the party is a reason to run.

**Age.** The higher the rank, the longer a person lives. A Silver-ranker who looks forty may have seen two centuries.
