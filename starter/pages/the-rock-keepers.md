A monster cult among the fishing families of the delta near [[Greenstone]]. They leave offerings on a rock no one will name, and the monsters leave their boats alone.

| Wants | Holds power through | Default view of a new Iron-ranker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Safe waters, and their god kept fed and hidden | Family loyalty, silence, a very large monster | A threat to the god, or an offering |

## Patron, want, cover, weakness

- **Patron.** An old, enormous marsh monster that protects the boats of those who feed it. They believe it is a god.
- **Want.** To keep it fed and keep the [[Adventure Society]] away from it.
- **Cover.** Ordinary fishing families with a local superstition.
- **Weakness.** It is getting hungrier. Fish and goats are no longer enough, and some of the families are starting to say so.

## Structure

The Rock Keepers are not an organisation. They are four fishing families in the delta channels north of [[Greenstone]], tied by marriage, who have fed the rock for as long as anyone remembers.

- **The Keeper.** The eldest of the families, who knows the prayers and the old ways. Now that is Oona Reedy.
- **The families.** Fishers, eel farmers and boatwrights: Reedys, Marrows, Fennicks and Coys. About sixty people, most of whom have never thought of it as a cult.
- **The young.** A new generation who have been to Greenstone, seen Adventure Society badges and started to ask why they need a god.

## How it operates

- **The offering.** On the dark of each moon, the families take a boat to the rock at dusk, with fish, goats and the year's first eels. Oona sings, the offering goes over the side, and the water boils.
- **The protection.** Boats marked with a painted hand on the prow are left alone by the marsh's monsters. The families' catches are the best in the delta, and their children don't drown.
- **The silence.** Nobody talks to outsiders about the rock. An inspector or adventurer who gets too curious has an accident in the channels.
- **The hunger.** In the last two years the Old One has wanted more. It took a fisherman's boy last summer. Oona told the families it was a sign of favour.

## Signs

- The families' boats come back with full nets when everyone else's are empty.
- A painted hand, faded and old, on the prow of every boat in four villages.
- Goats keep going missing from farms nearby, and the farmers blame wolves that the delta does not have.
- An Adventure Society survey team sent to map the northern channels came back three people short and refused to say what happened.
- Monster sightings near the families' waters are rare; just outside them they are very common.

## Lair: the rock

A black stone island no bigger than a house rises out of a wide, deep pool where four channels meet. Weed hangs from it, and old offerings rot on its ledges: bones, wooden charms, rusted fishhooks. The Old One sleeps in the mud beneath the pool. The water is deep, murky and full of its smaller servants: giant eels, snapping turtles and crocodiles. A boat that reaches the rock without the painted hand will be attacked long before it arrives.

## Members

- **Oona Reedy** (Iron), the Keeper. A tiny, ancient woman with a voice like reeds in the wind, who has fed the rock for fifty years. She loves her families and would give the Old One anyone to keep them safe, including herself, and including a child.
- **Tam Reedy** (Iron), her great-grandson, a young fisher who joined the Adventure Society against her wishes. He doubts, and he is frightened for his little sister, who Oona has started looking at the way she looks at offerings.
- **The Old One on the Rock**, the god: a vast, ancient turtle-like monster with a shell like a reef and a beak that can break a boat in half.

- **Water** (common, Water): Flow, adaptation and patient force. Grants **Water-Finder**. The essence of water in all its patience. Bearers are rarely flustered, droplets bead on their skin even in dry air, and their magic sounds like distant rain.
- **Mire** (uncommon, Water): Sucking mud and treacherous ground. Grants **Ground Sense**. Mire is the patient grip of bog and marsh, which holds on to whatever it takes. Bearers walk with a heavy, deliberate tread, their boots never quite dry, and their magic seeps out like dark water.
- **Shepherd** (uncommon, Life): Guidance, care and tending flocks. Grants **Flock Keeper**. Shepherd is the watchful care of those who guide and protect others. Bearers have a calm, low voice and a habit of counting heads, and animals drift toward them as if expecting to be led.
- **Confluence: Reef** (Water + Mire + Shepherd): Living coral sheltering the sea's young. Grants **Nursery Waters**. Reef is the teeming garden of the shallow sea, a living wall where countless small things are sheltered and mended. A bearer's skin carries faint coral-pink and turquoise mottling, tiny shells catch in their hair, and their spells shimmer like sunlight through clear water full of darting fish.

- **Fish** (common, Water): Swimming, schools and deep water. Grants **Deep Lungs**. The essence of the fish. Faint scales glint at bearers' wrists and neck, they feel more at ease in water than out of it, and gill-like slits flutter at their throat when they hold their breath.
- **Harbour** (uncommon, Water): Safe haven, tides and ships. Grants **Tide Sense**. Harbour is the safety of a port after a long voyage. Bearers smell faintly of salt and tar, walk with a sailor's easy roll, and always seem to know when a storm is coming.
- **Spear** (common, Might): Reach, thrusting and holding the line. Grants Proficiency with pikes or with tridents. The essence of the spear, the oldest weapon of hunters and soldiers alike. - **Confluence: Tsunami** (Fish + Harbour + Spear): The sea rising with crushing force. Grants **Rising Wave**. The tsunami is the ocean's full strength, the wall of water that rises without warning and sweeps everything before it. Bearers carry a heavy, rolling stillness and the water in nearby cups trembles when they are angry, and their spells surge outward like a breaking wave.

**Languages** understands the Rock Keepers' prayers but cannot speak  
**Challenge** 12 (8,400 XP) · **Proficiency Bonus** +4

#### Traits

***Amphibious.*** It can breathe air and water.

***Mud Hide.*** While it is submerged in water or mud and doesn't move, it is indistinguishable from a sandbar or a rock.

***Lord of the Marsh.*** Marsh monsters of Bronze rank or lower within a mile will not attack a boat marked with the Rock Keepers' sign, and will come when it bellows.

#### Actions

***Multiattack.*** It makes one bite attack and two flipper attacks.

***Bite.*** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +11 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. *Hit:* 33 (4d12 + 7) piercing damage, and a Large or smaller target is grappled (escape DC 19).

***Flipper.*** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +11 to hit, reach 15 ft., one target. *Hit:* 20 (3d8 + 7) bludgeoning damage, and a boat it hits takes the same damage.

***Swallow.*** It swallows a creature it has grappled. A swallowed creature is blinded and restrained, and takes 21 (6d6) acid damage at the start of each of its turns. Dealing 30 damage to it from inside makes it regurgitate everything.

***Undertow (Recharge 5–6).*** It dives and rolls. Each creature in water within 30 feet must succeed on a DC 19 Strength saving throw or be pulled 20 feet toward it and dragged underwater. Boats within 30 feet capsize on a failed DC 15 check by whoever is steering.

</div>

**Using the Old One.** It is far stronger than an Iron party. They should not fight it head on. Better choices: break the families' faith, starve it until it comes looking, call in a Silver team, or find out why it has grown so hungry. A monster with a Silver core near the delta is a fortune and a disaster in one.
