> The full player handout: what any well-informed resident of Greenstone would know. Safe to share with players.

## Welcome to Pallimustus

Pallimustus is a world where magic is as common as weather, monsters rise from the land itself, and every person's power can be measured. The gods answer prayers. Adventurers carry licenses. And most people live their whole lives without ever learning how much of the world is hidden from them.

You begin as one of those ordinary people. What you become is up to you.

This primer tells you what any well-informed resident of the city of Greenstone would know. It is not the whole truth. Some of what follows is common knowledge, some is rumor, and some is simply what people prefer to believe. Finding out which is which is part of the adventure.

> *"Everyone in Greenstone knows the rules. Very few know who wrote them."* — a saying in the Old City

## How power works

Power on Pallimustus is not a secret. It is ranked, licensed, and visible to anyone trained to sense it. Most people are Normal. Those who rise above it do so through **essences**.

### Ranks

| Rank | At the table | How the world sees it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Level 1 | Ordinary people: farmers, clerks, merchants, most of the city, including those with one or two essences |
| Iron | Levels 2–4 | A full set of essences; new adventurers, taking local contracts and proving themselves |
| Bronze | Levels 5–10 | Respected professionals who handle regional threats |
| Silver | Levels 11–16 | Powers in their own right; they age slowly and are rarely refused |
| Gold | Levels 17–20 | Figures who shape the fate of cities and kingdoms |
| Diamond | Beyond | Spoken of more often than seen |

The higher the rank, the longer a person lives. A Silver-ranker who looks forty may have seen two centuries. It is wise to be polite to anyone whose age you cannot guess.

### Essences

An essence is a physical thing: a small object of condensed magic, often taken from a slain monster or sold for a fortune. Absorb one and it bonds to you for life, reshaping what you can do.

- **Each essence has a theme**, such as Dark, Blood, Fire, Swift or Might. It colors how your abilities look and feel.
- **You can hold three.** When the third bonds, the three combine into a fourth, the **confluence**, which forms at once. No one can fully predict what a confluence will be. Choose your three carefully.
- **Essences are found, not given.** You do not gain them by growing stronger; you gain them by earning, buying, finding or taking them. Your DM will see that opportunities arise as you grow.

At the table, each essence grants either proficiency in one skill or tool that fits its theme, or one cantrip that fits its theme. It also lets your existing abilities take on its look. A Dark-essence rogue's strikes trail shadow; a Fire-essence fighter's blade glows like a forge.

### Awakening stones

An essence is potential. **Awakening stones** unlock it. These rare stones, found in monster lairs and strange places, bond to a matching essence and teach it a new ability. At the table, each one grants a spell tied to that essence that you can cast once per long rest without a slot. You can hold a number of these abilities equal to your proficiency bonus.

### Money and monster cores

The world runs on **spirit coins**: magic made solid. Hold one and it feels like glass, though it shines like metal. Taste one and it is like touching your tongue to lightning. They are money, and they are also something far more useful.

A coin is worth exactly as much as the magic inside it. Iron coins are cloudy and pale blue, magic only half-refined. Each rank above is purer, clearer and rarer, and worth a hundred coins of the rank below. Mundane coin still changes hands at market stalls, but no adventurer keeps their savings in it.

| Coin | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Iron spirit coin | 1 gp |
| Bronze spirit coin | 100 gp |
| Silver spirit coin | 10,000 gp |
| Gold spirit coin | Seldom seen outside treasuries |

**More than money.** Spirit coins are spent in more ways than one, and once used, they are gone.

- **Fuel.** Rituals, enchantments and many magic items burn spirit coins the way lamps burn oil. At the table, coins of equal value can stand in for any costly spell component.
- **Food.** Those who carry essences can live on magic instead of bread. A coin of your own rank replaces a day's food and water. Familiars and other magical creatures eat them gladly, and some eat little else.
- **A desperate edge.** Swallow a coin one rank above your own and, for a moment, you are something more. At the table, as a bonus action, you gain advantage on Strength, Dexterity and Constitution checks and saving throws for 1 minute, then gain one level of exhaustion. Anything stronger than that, people say, is not worth surviving.
- **Thin places.** In lands where magic runs thin, the strong grow weak without coins to sustain them. At the table, if you do not eat a coin of your rank each day there, you regain no Hit Dice on a long rest.

**Where they come from.** Spirit coins are made in **coin farms**: fortified mints built where the magic runs thickest. Here is the trouble: the same magic that makes coins makes monsters. The richest farms sit in the most dangerous places in the world, and guarding them is steady work for adventurers. Whoever controls a farm controls a mint, and in Greenstone, that question is never as settled as it looks.

**Monster cores.** Slain monsters leave cores behind: small, gem-like stones, red-brown in the weakest monsters. Crafters and ritualists prize them. And some people eat them, though not for dinner.

### Two paths to power

There are two ways to rise in rank, and everyone in Greenstone has an opinion about which is respectable.

**The hard way.** Use your powers, all of them, in real danger, over and over. It is slow, it hurts, and it kills people. Those who survive it know exactly what they can do. At the table, this is ordinary 5e leveling.

**The easy way.** Absorb monster cores. No danger, no training, just money. With a full enough purse, a person can reach Bronze rank without ever seeing a monster, and many of Greenstone's wealthy and well-born have done exactly that. At the table, you gain a level by absorbing cores of your current rank, a number equal to the new level. Iron cores cost about 50 gp; Bronze cores, about 5,000 gp; Silver cores, far more.

Adventurers call the easy way a trap, and they have their reasons:

- **It closes the other door.** Every core makes honest growth a little harder. At the table, each level gained through cores permanently cuts the XP you earn by 25%. After a fourth core level, you can grow only by cores.
- **Power without practice.** Core users hold power they never learned to wield. At the table, once you have gained any level through cores, your awakening-stone spells have −2 to attack rolls and save DCs.
- **It leaves something behind.** Those with the senses for it say they can tell a core user at a glance, and the Church of Purity can always tell. No one who climbed by cores has ever risen past Silver (level 16). As to why, people say a great many things. The Church calls it corruption. Others whisper that something lingers in the soul, and that somewhere, someone knows how to wash it out.

The choice is yours. Just make it knowing what it costs.

## Your character

You play under ordinary 5e rules. Build your character with any class, background and level-1 options as normal. Essences add to that character; they never replace it.

- **You start at Normal rank.** At level 1 you may have no essence, or one or two. You are skilled, perhaps trained, but still one of the many. You become Iron rank when all your slots are full: three essences and the confluence they form. That is the moment the world starts to take you seriously.
- **You level normally.** Experience and milestones work as usual in 5e. Essences arrive through play, and the DM paces them to your growth. A rough expectation is that you fill your set, and reach Iron, around level 2. After that you grow through levels and awakening stones, not more essences.
- **Think about your themes early.** Ask yourself what your character would reach for if offered power. Essences people speak of include Dark, Blood, Sin, Fire, Water, Earth, Wind, Light, Magic, Swift and Might. Others exist, and some are rarely spoken of at all.

### Where you come from

Most characters are natives of Pallimustus, drawn from one of its peoples (see *Peoples of Pallimustus*). If you want to play a species not found there, such as a dwarf, halfling, gnome, tiefling or orc, your character is an **outworlder**.

Outworlders arrive from other worlds. How they come, and why, is not widely understood. Each is a one-off: you are the only one of your kind anyone here has ever seen. You gain one extra language and start with no reputation at all, for better or worse. People who know what to look for may notice that you are not from here. Not all of them will be friendly.

A native species can also be an outworlder, if you want your character to discover this world alongside you.

## The world

### Magic in the land

Magic on Pallimustus is not only cast; it gathers. It pools in certain places the way water pools in low ground, and where it gathers thickly enough, **monsters** take shape. They are not animals. They do not breed or migrate. They simply appear, again and again, wherever the magic runs deep.

This is why adventurers exist. A land rich in magic is rich in danger, and someone has to keep it in check.

### Monster surges

Sometimes the magic builds faster than anyone clears it. Spawns grow more frequent, then stronger, then stranger, over weeks. Older residents speak of past **surges** the way coastal towns speak of storms: in lowered voices, with a list of the names they lost. No one can say for certain when the next one will come.

### Astral spaces

Scattered across the world are doorways to **astral spaces**: pocket realms with their own rules, their own inhabitants, and treasures found nowhere else. Some are charted and licensed. Some are known only to whoever found them first. A few have entrances no one has managed to close, and some have doors that no one has managed to open.

### The gods

The gods of Pallimustus are real, and they are not distant. Each embodies an idea, such as Healing, Knowledge, Death or Purity, and each has a church that speaks for it. Priests heal wounds, bless ventures, and sometimes receive answers. The gods are said to be bound by rules among themselves, which is perhaps why they act through mortals so often.

### Beyond the sky

Scholars speak of the **astral**, a vast space between worlds, and of beings who dwell there whose power makes the gods seem modest. Most people treat such talk as scripture or speculation. Those who have reason to know better tend not to discuss it.

## Greenstone and beyond

### Greenstone

Greenstone is your home, or the city you have just arrived in. It sits at the edge of delta country, where rivers braid through marsh and reed, and where the magic runs deep enough that monsters are a fact of daily life. That makes it a good city for adventurers, and a busy one.

- **The streets** are a mix of old stone and new enchantment. Magical lamps light the main roads, and some of the grander buildings seem to be made of shaped, solid cloud.
- **The Old City** holds the oldest districts, narrow streets, clinics, and people who remember how things used to be.
- **The great houses** keep estates where the city's real decisions are rumored to be made.
- **The guild halls** of the Adventure Society and the Magic Society are where contracts are posted, ranks are judged, and monster cores change hands.
- **The roads** carry two-headed, scaled beasts called heidels, which pull the carts and carry the riders. There are no horses here. Newcomers find this stranger than they expect.

### The delta and the marsh

Beyond the walls, the delta stretches out in channels and swamp. It is where most new adventurers earn their first contracts, and where many learn their first hard lessons. Locals will tell you which waterways to avoid. They will not always tell you why.

### The wider world

Greenstone answers to no king. Far to the south and west, an ocean away in warm island waters, lies the **Storm Kingdom**, ruled from the coastal capital of **Rimaros**. Rimaros is larger, richer, and far more political, a city where reputations are made at court as often as in the field. Ships trade between the two, and the Storm Kingdom's envoys are a familiar sight at the great houses.

Other great cities lie farther afield, and the maps grow less certain the farther you travel.

## Peoples of Pallimustus

Nine peoples call Pallimustus home, and all of them can take essences. Locals call them *natures*. In Greenstone you will meet most of them in a single afternoon. Here, rank and family matter far more than what you were born as.

| People | What they are known for | 5e species | In Greenstone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Humans | The most widespread people of the world | Human | Everywhere |
| Elves | Long lives and a natural gift for spellcraft and life magic | Elf | Common |
| Leonids | Large, lion-featured, proud; famed as crafters and fighters | Leonin, or reskinned Goliath | Common |
| Runics | Markings like runes that run down to the bone; deep reserves of magic | Reskinned Aasimar or Genasi | Uncommon |
| Celestines | A touch of the holy and the far-off about them; quick and gifted | Aasimar | Uncommon |
| Draconians | Scaled, with dragon-like gifts; they claim descent from dragons, and most people smile politely at the claim | Dragonborn | Uncommon |
| Smoulders | Fire-touched people, warm to the touch | Fire Genasi, or reskinned Tiefling | Uncommon |
| Goblins | A people with their own history and pride; calling one a monster is a quick way to make an enemy | Goblin | Uncommon |
| Merfolk | People of the seas and coasts | Water Genasi or Triton | Rare this far inland |

Each people has its own natural gifts. At the table, your 5e species traits represent them.

There are no dwarves, gnomes or halflings native to Pallimustus. If you meet one, you have met an outworlder, and you should probably ask how they got here.

## Powers in the city

Greenstone has no king. It is a city-state ruled by a Duke of the Mercer family, and it stands on three pillars: the **Adventure Society**, the **great houses** and the **churches**. Each does something different. The Duke keeps the streets, walls and courts running and spends most of his time keeping all three pillars happy. Below is what the street says about each power. The street is not always right.

### The Adventure Society

The Society licenses adventurers, posts contracts, judges rank, and disciplines those who break its rules. Every adventurer answers to it, at least officially. It claims to stand above politics and serve only the common good. Most people believe it, mostly. Getting your first license here is the start of every adventuring career, and the Society never forgets a name once it has one on file.

### The Magic Society

Scholars, enchanters and ritualists. The Magic Society buys monster cores, appraises essences and awakening stones, and researches magic no one else understands. It works alongside the Adventure Society because it must. The two are said to tolerate each other the way neighbors do when they share a wall.

### The churches

Every god has its faithful in Greenstone. The healing churches run clinics and are generally welcome everywhere. The **Church of Purity** is louder. Its faithful believe some kinds of magic, such as darkness, blood and death, are a corruption that should be cleansed. Those with such essences are wise to know where the Church's people gather, and how warmly they are likely to be received.

### The great houses

A noble house here is not a title from a king. It is the family of someone who became very powerful, usually an adventurer who reached Gold or Diamond rank and got rich doing it. They use that wealth to protect their own, with large estates, good gear and the best schooling for their children. Houses make deals and alliances with each other constantly. The **Gellers** are the best known. Doing a great house a favor can open doors for years. Crossing one can close them for longer.

### Foreign powers

The Storm Kingdom and other distant realms have no authority here, but they have money and envoys, and both are welcome at the right doors.

### What hides in the corners

Not every power in Greenstone keeps an office. There are always whispers: of people who vanished, of rituals in places no one should be, of officials who seem to know things they should not. Most of it is gossip. Most.

## Whispers

Every city has its rumors. Some of these are true. Some are half true. Some are exactly what someone wants you to believe.

- The magic in the delta has been running thicker this year. The old-timers have started counting.
- Some astral spaces were not found. They were *made*, and whoever made them may not be finished with them.
- A confluence is not random. Somewhere, someone understands exactly how it is chosen.
- Spirit coins can do more than buy things. The strongest adventurers do not spend theirs.
- Outworlders do not arrive by accident.
- Not everyone who wears a priest's robes serves the god they pray to.
- The gods are bound by rules. Not everything above them is.
- Some essences are never sold in any market, and the people who carry them do not say where they came from.

### Questions no one in Greenstone can answer

1. What really causes a surge?
2. Where do outworlders come from, and why do they come here?
3. What lives in the astral, and what does it want with worlds like this one?
4. Who wrote the rules the gods obey?

Perhaps you will be the one to find out.
