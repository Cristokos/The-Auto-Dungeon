**City-state.** A port city on the south-west coast, where the Mistrun River spreads into delta and marsh before it reaches the sea, with desert on every landward side. Greenstone answers to no king: it is ruled by its Duke, from the Mercer family. The magic here is thin, so its monsters are weaker than most, which makes it a good place for new adventurers and a frustrating one for ambitious ones. The starting hub for Iron-rank play.

## What players know

Magic is everywhere in the world, but it does not run evenly. In Greenstone it is thin: rituals take longer, high-rank magic can sputter, and the monsters that spawn in the delta are mostly Iron and Bronze. That makes the city safe enough to live in and busy enough to fight in. Bronze-rankers with big ambitions tend to leave for richer magic. Plenty of Iron-rankers never do.

- **Two cities in one.** The **[[The Old City|Old City]]** is the original Greenstone: canals, warehouses, tenements and the working port, built in desert stone. **[[The Island]]** is a newer artificial island off the shore, first raised as a breakwater and now home to the rich, the guilds and the temples. A toll bridge and the Loop, a subway that runs under the water, join the two.
- **Green stone.** The city is named for the green marble quarried in the hills inland and shipped all over the world. It paves the Island, faces its grand buildings and gives the river boats their colour.
- **The Duke.** Greenstone is a city-state, and the [[The Duke of Greenstone|Duke]] rules it from the Ducal Palace with a household guard as formidable as any in the city. The Duke is a Mercer, which makes [[House Mercer]] the most politically powerful family in Greenstone, whatever the Gellers think.
- **The three pillars.** The Duke rules, but cannot rule against the [[Adventure Society]], the [[Noble and Adventuring Houses|great houses]] and [[The Churches]] all at once. Most of the city's real politics is the Duke balancing the three.
- **The guild halls.** The [[Greenstone Adventure Society Branch|Adventure Society]] and the [[Magic Society]] keep their halls in the Guild District on the Island, where contracts are posted, ranks are judged and monster cores change hands.
- **The water.** Barges and green-stone riverboats work the Mistrun under magic lamps and arched bridges. Trade ships crowd the Old City port, and the marinas hold everything from fishing boats to the floating cloud houses of the very rich.
- **The roads** carry two-headed, scaled beasts called heidels, which pull the carts and carry the riders. There are no horses here. Newcomers find this stranger than they expect.
- **The food.** Chittle kebabs from street grills are cheap, everywhere and, according to visitors, a test of character.

> *Everyone in Greenstone knows the rules. Very few know who wrote them.* (a saying in the Old City)

## Region

Greenstone sits where [[The Delta and Marshes|the Mistrun delta]] meets the sea. The delta's channels and farms run north and east of the city, the river leads upstream into the desert, and beyond the delta the desert runs inland for weeks. The river is fed by a great waterfall that pours out of an [[Astral Spaces|astral space]] aperture in the hills, at [[North East Quarry Village Four]]. Ships and the odd sky ship trade with distant [[Rimaros]] in [[The Storm Kingdom]], and two or three astral space entrances lie within a few days' travel.

## Filling the gaps

Build each nearby region with three questions:

1. **How much ambient magic does it have?** High-magic regions spawn more and stronger monsters, attract adventurers, and carry surge risk.
2. **Who protects it?** A city with a strong Adventure Society branch is safe; a remote town relying on one Bronze-ranker is fragile.
3. **Who profits from it?** Monster-rich land is valuable for cores, materials and [[Spirit Coins|spirit coin farms]], so nobles, churches and guilds contest it.

## DM notes

**Low magic, in numbers.** Greenstone has ordinary amounts of magic but of poor quality, so magic is reliable for everyday use but weak for anything big. Rituals take noticeably longer than they should, and anything that draws on the surrounding magic for extra power is slower and less dependable. Monster cores help balance a ritual. Iron-rank spawns are common and Bronze spawns are regular. Silver spawns are rare enough to be a city-wide event.

**Low standards.** Because the threats are small, the branch's training standards are low. Many Iron-rankers here stall out and never reach Bronze, and the Bronze-rankers looking to advance head for higher-magic cities. A disciplined team stands out quickly.
:::
