**Kingdom.** A coastal and island realm ruled from [[Rimaros]], with a royal family and powerful noble houses. The national politics backdrop. See [[The Crown]].

**What players know.** A warm, rich island kingdom an ocean away from [[Greenstone]]. It has no say in Greenstone's affairs; the two trade by ship, and the Storm Kingdom's envoys are a familiar sight at the great houses.

**Law.** Slavery is legal in the Storm Kingdom. Slaves are bought and sold openly, often bound by magical contracts, and debtors can be sold to cover what they owe. The [[Church of Dominion]] blesses the trade; the [[Church of Liberty]] works against it, often outside the law. The [[Church of Undeath]] is outlawed.

## The land

The kingdom is a long chain of islands strung along a warm coast, with the mainland shore at its western end. The sea between them is shallow, bright and full of reefs, and nearly every journey in the kingdom is made by boat.

- **The Crown Isle.** The largest island, home to [[Rimaros]]: terraced farmland, sugar and coffee estates in the hills, and the palace above the harbour. Everything that matters in the kingdom passes through here.
- **The Windward Isles.** A string of small, rocky islands facing the open ocean, battered by storms in the wet season. Fishing towns, lighthouses, coral forts and naval stations watching toward [[The Drowning Compass]].
- **The Sugar Coast.** The mainland shore at the kingdom's western end, cleared for vast plantations worked by slaves. The richest land in the realm, and the place where the [[Church of Liberty]] is most active.
- **The Green Teeth.** Jungle mountains running down the spine of the mainland coast. Cloud forest, waterfalls, ruins older than the kingdom, and astral space entrances nobody has fully mapped. Monsters there are stronger and stranger than the coast, and the Adventure Society posts Silver contracts for them year round.
- **The Salt Cays.** Hundreds of low sandy islets in the shallow east, with salt pans, turtle beaches and smugglers' coves. Nobody can patrol them all, and nobody really tries.

## The great houses

The royal family is Celestine, but most of the kingdom's nobles are not. Old human and elven families hold the islands for the crown in return for taxes, ships and adventurers.

- **The island lords.** Each island or stretch of coast is held by a noble house that collects its taxes, raises its militia and keeps a seat at court. The richest are the plantation houses of the Sugar Coast, whose wealth rests on slave labour.
- **The admiralty houses.** A handful of families have commanded the royal fleet for generations. They control the shipyards and the navy, and the crown needs them more than it likes.
- **Royal cousins.** Minor branches of the Celestine royal family hold estates of their own. Every one of them hopes to put a candidate into the next succession contest.
- **Merchant princes.** Trading families from Rimaros harbour who are not noble by birth but are richer than most who are. They buy titles when they can and marry into houses when they can't.

## Law and custom

- **The Storm King's law** runs on every island, enforced by royal magistrates and the island lords' militia. Justice is quick and harsh for commoners, slower and gentler for nobles.
- **Slavery** is woven through everything. Slave contracts are sealed by priests of [[Church of Dominion|Dominion]] and enforced by magic: a slave who breaks one suffers for it. Debtors, criminals and captives taken at sea can all be sold. Freed slaves carry papers they must show on demand.
- **Faith.** The [[Church of the Storm]] is honoured above all others and has priests at every court. Dominion is openly powerful. The [[Church of Undeath]] is outlawed, and so is any cult of an astral power. Liberty is legal but watched, and its priests are arrested often.
- **Adventurers** are respected and well paid, and the Adventure Society's standards are far stricter than Greenstone's. A Greenstone Iron-ranker is often treated as a beginner here until they prove otherwise.
- **The succession.** When the Storm King chooses to step down, royal candidates compete in a contest of deeds, and the king names the successor. Every candidate needs allies, victories and stories, and a successful foreign team can be all three.

## Trade

The kingdom sells sugar, rum, coffee, dyewood, pearls, salt and slaves, and buys grain, iron, cloth and magic. Ships to [[Greenstone]] carry rum and sugar out and green marble and delta rice home. Cloud-flask merchants in Rimaros harbour build sky ships for anyone who can afford one.

## DM notes

- **Using the kingdom.** It is the place the party goes when Greenstone is too small for them: bigger monsters, stricter standards, court politics and a moral problem they cannot ignore.
- **Slavery as a pressure.** A player character may be offered a slave as payment, see a debtor sold at auction, or find that a contract's client keeps slaves. Liberty will ask for help. There is no clean way through, and that is the point.
- **The succession** is the kingdom's great engine of plot. A royal candidate may hire the party to win a deed, steal one from a rival, or simply survive one.

**Hooks.**
- A royal candidate wants a Greenstone team, nobody in Rimaros, to clear a ruin in the Green Teeth before her cousin does. Her cousin has hired a team too.
- A slave ship bound for the Sugar Coast has been taken by Liberty rebels and run aground in the Salt Cays. The owner offers a contract to recover his property; the rebels offer a better one.
- A Windward lighthouse keeper reports a light out on the Drowning Compass where there is no island. The navy wants it investigated quietly.
- An admiralty house is holding back ships to pressure the crown. The party is asked to carry a royal message through a blockade that officially does not exist.
- Two plantation houses are at feud over a stretch of the Sugar Coast, and both are hiring adventurers. The slaves on the disputed land are planning to leave in the confusion.
