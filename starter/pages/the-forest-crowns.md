**Kingdoms.** A cluster of forest kingdoms in the rainforest and savannah inland from [[Yaresh]], on the southern continent. Each is ruled by its own king or queen from a stool of gold, and together they are rich, proud, and rarely at peace with each other for long.

**What players know.** Gold, kola nuts, timber and monster parts flow from the Forest Crowns down the rivers to Yaresh, and from there to the world. The kingdoms are famous for their goldsmiths, their drummers, their weavers and their warriors. Visitors who respect the courts are welcomed warmly. Visitors who don't seldom come back.

## The land

- **The Deep Forest.** Rainforest along the coast side of the kingdoms: giant trees, rivers the colour of tea, and a canopy so thick that noon looks like dusk. Its monsters are many, and some are very old.
- **The Gold Hills.** Forested hills in the west where the gold is mined. The richest kingdoms sit here, and so do the most bandits.
- **The Great River.** A wide river and its lake run through the east of the realm, carrying canoes and barges down to the sea.
- **The Northern Savannah.** Open grassland and scattered trees to the north, where the forest gives way to herds, horsemen and the trade roads north across the desert.

## The courts

There are seven Forest Crowns and many lesser chiefdoms that pay tribute to them.

- **The kings and queens** sit on golden stools, which are believed to hold the soul of the kingdom. A ruler can be removed by the council of elders; the stool cannot.
- **The queen mothers** choose the candidates for the throne, and have the right to rebuke a ruler in public. Many of them are more powerful than the rulers they choose.
- **The elders** of each kingdom's great families sit in council and advise the throne.
- **The linguists** speak for the ruler, who never speaks directly to a stranger. A visitor's words are repeated, softened and passed on. What reaches the throne can be very different from what was said.
- **The war companies** are sworn adventuring bands who serve the crown, hunt monsters and fight the kingdom's wars.

## Law and custom

- **The golden stool** is sacred. Insulting a kingdom's stool is an act of war.
- **Gold** belongs to the crown when it is found in the ground. Nuggets are crown property; dust may be kept by the miner. Smuggling gold is punished severely.
- **Slavery** is practised in some kingdoms as a punishment and for captives of war, and outlawed in others. It is a constant source of argument between the crowns, and with Yaresh.
- **Faith.** Ancestors are honoured in every home. Shrines to the forest's spirits stand at every river crossing, and the churches have a foothold mainly in the trade towns.

## Trade

The Forest Crowns sell gold, kola, timber, kente cloth, ivory and monster parts, and buy salt, horses, metal goods and magic. Yaresh handles most of the trade and takes a share of everything.

## DM notes

- **Using the Forest Crowns.** They are a place of courts and protocol, where the party must speak through a linguist and earn standing before they are trusted. Their monsters are old and cunning, and their politics are older still.
- **Rivalries.** Two or three of the crowns are always at odds. A party working for one will be watched by the others.

**Hooks.**
- A kingdom's golden stool has been stolen, and the kingdom is weeks from war with its neighbour, which it blames. The real thief is a cult from Yaresh.
- A queen mother wants a foreign team to escort her chosen candidate through the Deep Forest to a shrine where the ancestors will judge him.
- The gold mines of one hill have begun to produce cores instead of gold. Nobody knows where the monsters are.
- A Yaresh trading house is quietly arming one crown against another, and the party is hired to deliver the crates.
:::
