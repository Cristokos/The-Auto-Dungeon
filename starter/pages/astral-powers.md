Out in the astral, the vast space between worlds, dwell beings whose power makes the gods seem modest. The greatest of them are the **great astral beings**: cosmic forces that tend to every world at once, each promoting one part of how reality works. Like the gods, each keeps to its own domain and none trespasses on another's. Unlike the gods, they cannot touch a physical world at all; everything they do in one, they do through people. All of them belong at the far horizon of the campaign. On day one they are myth, scripture, and warnings from the very old.

**What players know.** Scholars speak of the astral and of the great beings in it, and a few, like [[Clive Standish]], venerate one openly. Most people treat such talk as scripture or speculation. Those who have reason to know better tend not to discuss it.

## How they act

A great astral being cannot step into a world, so it recruits.

- **Prime avatar.** Each great astral being has a prime avatar, also called its prime vessel: a Diamond-ranker who carries a seed of the being's power and acts as its anchor in physical reality. A prime avatar keeps their own mind, name and life, and serves across many worlds. When the being needs to speak or act directly, it can fill its prime avatar like a hand fills a glove, and the avatar's body is its only way to do so. The office can pass from one servant to the next.
- **Star seeds.** A great astral being can plant a seed of its power in a mortal. The seed grows, changes the host's body a little or a great deal, and makes them its puppet. Star seeds are far beyond Diamond rank, and ordinary cleansing magic cannot touch them.
- **Followers.** Most of a great astral being's influence comes from mortals who share its ideals and act on them freely: scholars, orders, cults and the occasional devoted adventurer.

Other names appear in the oldest texts with little attached to them: the **Seeker of Songs**, the **Whisper in Corners**, the **Word in the Silence**, the **Keeper of Songs**, and the **Nameless**, which may be one being or several. Scholars also note one strange gap. Destiny is the only great idea that no great astral being is said to govern.

## Astral kings and Messengers

Not every astral power is a great astral being. **Astral kings** are lesser beings of terrible power, each ruling a domain of the astral. **Messengers** are radiant, winged beings of the astral who see mortals as cattle, tools or worshippers. Messengers are the astral threat most likely to reach a mortal world, and the oldest stories of Pallimustus describe them coming once before.

:::dm
**Running the astral powers.** Keep them at the far horizon. A great astral being should never be fought, only bargained with, served or survived, and its notice should feel like weather changing. Prime avatars can appear in late Gold or Diamond play.

- **The rule they all live under.** Great astral beings answer to an order above them, sometimes called the Throne, and that order forbids them from acting in physical reality. It is why they need vessels, cults and seeds, and why a great astral being caught breaking the rules can be removed.
- **The World-Phoenix** is the likeliest ally. It guards the boundaries between worlds and will back mortals who protect them.
- **The Reaper** is neutral and absolute. It wants death to stay final, which sets it against undeath, resurrection tricks and anyone who traps souls.
- **The Builder** is the campaign's long shadow. It wants astral spaces carved off worlds, and [[The Builder Cult]] carves them for it. See [[The Builder]] for its secret origin.
- **The others** are colour. Use them for scholars, strange blessings and outworlder backstories.
- **The interstice.** Prime avatars often live in a place between worlds called the Interstice, where vessels of different beings meet on neutral ground.
:::
